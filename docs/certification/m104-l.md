### POSIX residual and qualification

Without a pidfd/native signal handle, Linux and Darwin still have a window
between the final exact kernel birth read and kill. The process may exit and
its numeric PID may be reused within that interval; Darwin's helper-return
and scheduling time is part of it. Mitigation: validate the exact birth and
current registry epoch, then call the synchronous per-PID signal without

### Finding 1: a second Stop during owner exit failed with `connect ENOENT`

Root cause (`src/runtime/lsp/supervisor.ts`): after answering `STOPPED`, the
supervisor's `finally` closes the control server (the socket path is
unlinked), waits up to `graceMs` for peers, and only then removes its owner
record. A Stop that reads the record in that window sees a live owner PID, a
record and no socket, and `stopWorkspace` rejected with the raw connect error.
Any double Stop (two editors, a repeated command) can hit it.

Fix (`5e08c1f51`): `stopWorkspace` treats `ENOENT`/`ECONNREFUSED` on the
control socket as an exiting owner and polls every `pollMs` within `exitMs`.
Liveness is sampled before the record check, so a record still present after
death is final. A removed record resolves the Stop. An owner that died and
left its record goes through the existing dead-owner receipt verification
(`UnverifiedStopError` without a matching completed receipt). Neither case
reaches the deadline, which rethrows the original error. `verifyTrustedPath`
and every export are unchanged.

Regression tests (`runtimeSupervisor.test.ts`, synthetic owner record whose
socket is already closed):

- `waits out an owner that closed its control socket and is removing its
record`: live owner (`process.pid`), record removed after 200 ms.
- `refuses an owner that closed its control socket and died keeping its
record`: owner child SIGKILLed after 200 ms, record kept.

### Finding 2: the native lifecycle helper could not link on Ubuntu 26.04

Root cause: `treesLifecycleNative.test.ts` compiled `resourceLifecycle.c`
through `runTreeProgram` with its default environment `{ LC_ALL: 'C' }` (no
PATH). gcc 15 here has no private `ld` (`/usr/x86_64-linux-gnu/bin` is
absent; `-print-prog-name=ld` gives a bare `ld` under an empty environment), so
collect2 could not find the linker. `env -i /usr/bin/cc …` reproduces it, and
`env -i PATH=/usr/bin:/bin /usr/bin/cc …` links. Fix (`5e08c1f51`): the three
compiles pass `COMPILE_ENV = { LC_ALL: 'C', PATH: '/usr/bin:/bin' }`. Only the
compiler gets the PATH. The spawned fixtures keep `env: {}`.
