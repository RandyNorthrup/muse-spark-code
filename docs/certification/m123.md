## Lane A redesign II — review RVM123A4, one logout barrier (2026-10-09)

Worktree `C:\Users\Randy\Coding\mx-m123a5`, branch `m123/a5` from `m123/a4`

The fifth review round on `src/runtime/github/deviceFlowSource.ts`, so its
state was redesigned instead of patched (PLAN.md M123, "Lane A review
RVM123A4: one logout barrier", which replaces the old state table).
RVM123A4's P1 came from two stored values (the generation and the
sign-out count) read at different times and combined to let a rotation
through, and from a Sign out of two writes; its P2 from that recovery not
knowing what had been refused. Both existed only because a refusal moved
the generation. Now:

- **One barrier.** Only Sign out writes the generation: a fresh random id,
  one write, read in one read. The sign-out count
  (`GITHUB_DEVICE_FLOW_SIGN_OUT_EPOCH_KEY`) and the recovery are gone.
- **Read once.** A Read (with its refresh) and a sign-in read the
  generation when they begin, and their pair (`GITHUB_STORED_TOKEN_VERSION`
  3, with a random `id`) names that generation and no other. Publish
  writes nothing once it has moved, and readers and Serve accept a pair
  only under the current generation.
- **A refusal touches only the refused pair.** It notes the pair's `id`
  (`GITHUB_DEVICE_FLOW_REFUSED_KEY`, `museSpark.github.deviceFlow.refused`,
  no secret) and deletes it while it is still that pair. A rotation
  replaces only the pair it began from, or the slot that pair's own
  refusal emptied; a sign-in is stored in its generation whatever the slot
  holds.
- **In one source,** a count of its Sign outs (memory only) refuses at
  Serve anything begun before one, and no caller joins a run begun before
  one. It only ever refuses.

### Residuals (also in PLAN.md M123)

- **Overlap, another process.** A Serve whose last generation read the
  store answered before another process's Sign out wrote its generation
  can return its bearer after that Sign out returned: its check came
  first. A store with no lock or notification cannot close it; in one
  source the Sign-out count does (D7).
- **Windows of two store calls in another process** (no compare-and-swap):
  Publish's checks and its write; a take-back's compare and its delete;
  Refuse's compare and its delete; Sign out's write and its delete. Each
  loses a pair (that process signs in again); none serves a pair issued
  before a Sign out.
- **A crash** after Sign out's generation write, or between a write and
  its take-back, leaves an unreadable pair until the next sign-in
  overwrites it or the next Sign out deletes it.
- **A refresh whose last caller cancels** after GitHub rotated the pair
  loses the rotation (the user signs in again), as before.
