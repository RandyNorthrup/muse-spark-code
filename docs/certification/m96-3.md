# C-M96-3 round 7: request lifetime owns late admission

Rig: macbook. Worktree: CM963R4, branch `c/m96-3`.

## Finding and fix

Cancellation can abandon a compaction stream while durable worker admission
continues. Cleanup previously found no callback; its late completion attached
an unsent claim to the ended request. Round 6's local completion statement
below is historical and superseded by this review and repair.

`ModelApiHost.responseAttemptGuard` captures its turn, compaction controller
and owner token. After prior settlement and caller preparation it checks that
same lifetime before further admission. When reservation returns after Stop,
replacement or close, it awaits the claim's zero-cost release and throws
before attachment. The engine port independently rechecks after pending meter
writes, and after durable reservation it releases its exact ordinal before
returning a callback if worker admission expired. Its existing consumed flag
makes the terminal callback idempotent. No dispatch marker, ambiguous liability,
price, public setting, wire schema or paid permission changed.

- The integrated outcome table runs actual host, transport, engine port and
  team service with a held state write. Cancel and close during compaction
  produce one POST, one billed receipt ($0.00002775), a second claim closed
  at zero, no open claims, and matching day/lifetime totals. A test-only session
  port forwards the terminal report after real compaction; all admission and
  accounting use production code and fake captured API replies.

The worker callback's only producer/consumer paths are now covered. Ordinary
`persistReservation` already releases when its open-reservation identity ended
during the save. Subagents use that same journal and release a late resource
lease before launch. Best-of-N checks run/attempt lifetime after resource and
driver admission; its catch/disposal owns cleanup. Hook requests retain their
local claims through awaited admission and unconditional `finally` settlement;
they do not abandon that ownership with compaction's stream race. Paid-daily
admission checks its signal after reservation and each later wait, refunds on
failure, and schedule admission refunds both claims. Media and image paths
retain local/finally ownership and conservative dispatched liability. None has
a sibling worker callback attached after abandoned cleanup.

# C-M96-3 round 6: per-attempt worker accounting (historical)

Rig: macbook. Worktree: CM963R4, branch `c/m96-3`.
Reviewed base: `8bd3744712122803fa22ce6910c9f992dc93a10a`.
Review: RVCM963F, 2026-10-11. Both findings are one accounting class.

## Design and scope

The lead requires worker cost to settle per attempt through ordinary request
outcomes. `ResponseAttemptGuard.reserveUsage` returns the callback for one
attempt. The host owns it before final admission, marks dispatch adjacent to
transport, and closes it once at `endRequest`. Retry/rebuild preparation
awaits the prior outcome before a new reservation. Valid usage reports
`billed`; a dispatched attempt without valid usage reports `ambiguous`;
refusal or a failure before dispatch reports `released`. A later successful
retry never replaces the earlier ambiguous attempt's full maximum.

The engine port maps those outcomes to the exact service ordinal and awaits
persistence. Final uncertain claims remain counted, labelled estimated, and
are closed rather than left reserved. Explicit Reset can clear completed
liability; open reservations still survive Reset. A revoked admission during
the reservation save removes the never-dispatched claim before returning.
The compaction wrapper preserves the same worker preparation and outcomes.
Cancellation cannot overwrite a proved 429 refusal, and a throwing
pre-fetch callback cannot mark the worker attempt dispatched.
No wire shape changes: this outcome is an internal accounting contract.

Changes stay in the engine accounting files. No dependency, guard widening,
threshold, timeout, hook, credential handling, paid/live call, UI styling,
merge, push or public team activation. No installs.
