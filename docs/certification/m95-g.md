## Wire truth

Every wire claim is one of the twelve captured frames in
`docs/certification/m95-captures/gemini/` (recorded 2026-10-04,
`gemini-3.5-flash-lite`), named in `docs/certification/m95-captures.md`
§ Google Gemini:

- The tool-call chunk carries `functionCall {name, args, id}` whole with
  `thoughtSignature` on the same part, then an empty text part with
  `finishReason: "STOP"` — so the codec detects calls by their parts, never
  by the reason.
- The follow-up replays the signed call plus `functionResponse {id, name,
  response}` as `user`, and was accepted — including its trailing empty
  `{text: ""}` model part, which is the scratchpad's construction, not a
  wire requirement, and which the codec does not reproduce.
- The final answer's empty text part carries a `thoughtSignature` that the
  captured replay omits — and is still accepted. The codec drops text-part
  signatures on decode for exactly this reason.
- Errors arrive as JSON under `content-type: text/event-stream`
  (`06-error-invalid-model.json`); `parseGeminiError` reads the body by
  content, and the 404 test pins the envelope verbatim.
- `usageMetadata` rides every chunk with cumulative counts; the last chunk
  wins. `cachedContentTokenCount` never appears, so the codec omits
  `cached_tokens` while absent (unknown, not zero).
- The models list is stored trimmed (`bodySummary`); the parser test feeds
  its three verbatim sample entries.

Research (`docs/certification/m95-research.md` §1.6) supplies the rules no
capture could: signatures go back in the exact part unmerged, only the first
parallel call carries one, Gemini 3 answers an unsigned first call with a
400, and foreign history may use `skip_thought_signature_validator`. An
unsigned replayed call therefore takes the documented dummy — the one
uncaptured behavior in the codec, marked as such in the test.
