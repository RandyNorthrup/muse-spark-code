## Design as built

The workspace index contains one immutable, fsynced generation and a bounded
journal (64 operations, 256 KiB). A reserved final slot seals the old
generation before compaction. Activation atomically replaces `current.json`
and exclusively publishes a tiny generation-epoch fence. That fence, rather
than the mutable pointer hint, decides committed state even when an expired
writer resumes a pointer rename already in flight. Epoch fences are segmented
and carry no prompts. Old generations and interrupted staging are retired;
locked retirement is retried on later mutations without failing a commit.
Readers share the writer lease, so ordinary retirement starts after they finish.
Retirement retries a UUID directory even if a previous partial deletion
removed its headers before a locked child failed. The writer lease excludes
another live preparation; the current generation remains excluded by name.

Transient publication/activation/release errors have five bounded retries
with named backoff. Committed hard links are recognized by BigInt file
identity; completed publication cannot fail merely because temporary cleanup
was locked. Leases contain PID, process start, random token and heartbeat.
Lease reads use the same retry bound, rechecking their paths each attempt.
Dead or expired owners can be replaced, including failed releases while the
original PID remains alive. Token-scoped heartbeat files cannot overwrite a
replacement owner, even with an old rename already in flight. Unchanged
pulses expire by monotonic observation when wall time rolls backward. A
takeover verifies both lease and pulse before removal; guarded writes refuse
an owner that lost its lease. Disappearing destinations during exclusive
publication are a lost race, rather than a false publication failure.

Run identities, admission decisions, settled digests and applied markers live
in separate hash segments. Their retained content grows linearly, never
recopied with workspace state. Fences survive the schedule lifetime plus
eight days after removal. Permanent identifier tombstones prevent recycling
even after grace. Audit retains at most 100 fires per schedule and 30 days.
Completed migration receipts are archived separately from live state. These
identity and generation fences have no historical prompt payload.

Failure counters use a separate baseline and at most three outcome markers,
so audit expiry cannot lose consecutive failures and late settlements retain
admission chronology. The streak saturates at the pause threshold; fire
counts remain exact. Explicit counter edits reset this summary, and removal
erases it. Trigger/zone edits remove the old cursor lane, leaving at most one
per live schedule without reopening occurrence fences. Reserved object map
ids are refused before publication so JSON/zod cannot drop a job into empty
success. An applied marker distinguishes raw audit-before-index interruption
from completed settlement, without resurrecting expired audit on replay.
Completion-marker work stays in a separate bounded current-state queue until
its marker is physically published (maximum 1024, at most 32 per maintenance
pass). This committed evidence protects a successful index update if marker
publication fails and audit later expires. Published work retires during the
next record or maintenance mutation, avoiding a second transaction per fire.
Removed schedules retain their fences until completion work finishes.

An intent is durable before its occurrence fence. Each poll drains up to 32
pending runs, including living owners after transient advance/record errors.
The target durably admits a run id idempotently and exposes its ledger.
Absent means definitely unsent and safe to admit. Admitted means the target
owns recovery. Settled/uncertain carries final validated accounting;
ambiguous paid dispatch is never replayed. Durable acknowledgments preserve
known spend and retained liability even when final storage fails. An
uncertain ledger response must carry unknown cost certainty. A thrown
transport with admitted target work retains its intent until that target
returns final accounting, without completing a fictitious failed run.

Per-target dispatchers reread the persisted creation-time/id queue before
every pop. Parallel remains each schedule's explicit fresh-conversation
opt-in. Time admission compares the captured cursor/trigger/zone with current
state and rejects occurrences at or behind its committed cursor. Already
admitted recovery still accounts exactly once. Admission chronology is
independent of fire counts and wall-clock rollback.

Migration saves source identity/fingerprint, target identity/revision and
copied receipt counters before source removal. Reconciliation atomically
updates counters and receipt while preserving current authority and
legitimate v2 edits. A changed v1 job is refused; raced receipts reconcile.
A failed entry retains its source while later independent entries continue.

## Changed lane-0 contracts and integration bindings

- `ScheduleHostPort.lookupRun(runId)` is required. `deliver` must durably admit
  the id before sending and refuse duplicates across hosts/restarts. The
  target owns recovery of admitted work and exposes ambiguous sends as
  `uncertain`, with honest cost/liability and a person-visible final record.
  `uncertain` requires unknown cost certainty. No provider wire shape was
  invented: this is an internal target contract, not a service parser.
- `consecutiveFailures` saturates at the named pause threshold (three), with
  a bounded chronological summary rather than dependence on retained audit.
  `fireCount` remains exact. Store identifiers refuse the reserved record
  names `__proto__`, `constructor` and `prototype` before writing.
- The S-only run journal exposes durable `pending`, `acknowledge`,
  `acknowledged` and `maintain`; `advance` remains a boolean atomic decision.
  Its filesystem port adds guarded atomic replacement/exclusive writes and
  optional retirement retry. Migration has per-source receipt and atomic
  reconciliation methods. V1 source identities derive from its directory;
  original-job fingerprints are distinct from racing receipt fingerprints.
- T supplies real calendar/DST planning. E supplies persistent event
  readiness and identities. U/D bind paid/grant admission, durable target
  ledger and final accounting. The target ledger must retain identities
  at least as long as S's fences; S alone cannot guarantee a target that
  violates this contract. X/W bind the shared store/queue root, canonical
  workspace identity, ownership and lifecycle in every editor/runtime.
  RA owns model-free report execution. W owns shipping commands, localized
  surfaces, reference and the combined full-quality platform matrix.
- No new registered command, setting, dependency, locale key or escape hatch.
  S modules are not yet bound into shipped entry bundles. No paid/live call,
  credential read, install, push, merge, rebase or manual stash occurred.
  Counted model attempts: **0**.
